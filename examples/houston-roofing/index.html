What an inspection includes

We walk the roof. The driveway tells you nothing.

A proper inspection covers every slope on foot, or by drone where the pitch makes walking unsafe. We check the shingle field for bruising and lifted tabs, the ridge and hip caps, every pipe boot and piece of flashing, the valleys, the drip edge, the decking where it can be reached from the attic, and the gutters for granules that used to be on the roof. Hail leaves one pattern, wind another, age a third. We photograph all of it with a date stamp and you keep the photographs whether or not you hire us.

You get a written report the same week: what we found, what needs doing this season, what can wait, and what we would leave alone. If the roof is sound, the report says so.

What we do

  • Full replacement

    Tear-off to the deck, new synthetic underlayment, ice-and-water membrane in the valleys and around every penetration, then architectural shingle or standing-seam metal fastened to the manufacturer's high-wind pattern. The written estimate lists the products by manufacturer and line, the number of squares, the nailing pattern, a per-sheet price for any decking we find rotten at tear-off, the permit, disposal, and the warranty terms.

  • Storm repair

    Wind-lifted tabs, hail bruising, a limb through the deck. We tarp the same day where we can reach you, then repair or replace once the adjuster has seen it, so nothing is patched over before the claim is documented.

  • Insurance-claim inspection

    We inspect before you file and tell you plainly whether the damage will support a claim. If it will, we meet the adjuster on the roof with our photographs and measurements. You file the claim; it is your policy. Waiving a deductible is a criminal offence for a contractor in Texas, so we do not offer to.

  • Maintenance and small repairs

    A cracked pipe boot, a slipped ridge cap, a flashing reseal, a gutter that has pulled off the fascia. Small work in the spring that keeps a small leak from becoming a claim in September.

The insurance claim

Five steps, in the order they happen.

  1. Inspect before you file

    We look first. If the damage is cosmetic, or plainly below your deductible, we say so and your claim history stays clean.

  2. File with your carrier

    You make the call. We give you the photographs, the measurements and the date of the storm, so the claim is specific from the first conversation.

  3. Meet the adjuster

    We are on the roof when the adjuster is, with the same photographs. Your roof gets argued from the roof, where the damage is.

  4. Read the scope together

    The carrier sends a scope of loss with line items. We go through it with you, mark what is missing, and submit a supplement for anything the adjuster could not see that day.

  5. Build it, then close the claim

    We build to the approved scope, invoice for the recoverable depreciation, and hand you the paperwork your carrier needs to release the final payment.

Questions

How soon after a storm should the roof be looked at?

Within a few days, before the next storm moves the evidence around. Most policies give you a window to report damage; read yours, because the carrier sets that window.

Will filing a claim raise my premium?

We are not your insurer and cannot promise how they will rate you. What we can say is that a claim for a named storm is treated differently from a claim for neglect, and a documented inspection before filing is what makes that difference visible.

Can you waive my deductible?

No. Since 2019 it has been a criminal offence in Texas for a contractor to waive or absorb an insurance deductible, and a carrier can refuse the recoverable depreciation if it happens. You pay the deductible, and the invoice shows it.

What does the written estimate contain?

Products by manufacturer and line. The number of squares. The underlayment, the ventilation, and which flashing is replaced rather than reused. A per-sheet price for decking nobody can see until tear-off. The permit, disposal, and the warranty terms in writing. If a number is missing, ask for it.

Shingle or metal?

Both survive a Gulf hurricane when they are fastened correctly, and both fail when they are not. Architectural shingle costs less up front. Standing-seam metal lasts longer and sheds hail better, at a higher price. Ask and we will quote both.

Do you work outside Houston?

Harris, Fort Bend, Montgomery, Brazoria and Galveston counties. Farther out for a full replacement, case by case.
